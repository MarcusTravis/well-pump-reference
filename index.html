    Why this exists

    The U.S. Geological Survey estimates that around 43 million people in the United States get their household water from private domestic wells — wells owned and looked after by the household rather than a utility (USGS overview). Those households shop for pumps, tanks, and controls one brand site at a time. This library puts the specs side by side.

    Quick answers

    Submersible or jet? A jet pump sits above ground and lifts by suction, so a shallow-well jet works to about 25 ft; deeper wells usually take a submersible, which sits in the water and pushes it up. What size? Work out the gpm you need and the total head at that flow, then match a pump’s official curve. Which tank? Pick by drawdown, about one minute of pump run time per cycle. More in the FAQ and sizing basics.

    About this site

    The Well Pump Reference Library is free and has no ads, no affiliate links, and no tracking parameters in its links. Facts come from official manufacturer pages and catalog PDFs and from public retailer listings, last checked Nov 15, 2026. Notes are written in original words; pump curves and marketing text are never copied, only linked to the maker’s official page.

    Price rule. Price = lowest in-stock price for a new unit of that exact model seen on a public US retailer or manufacturer-store listing (seller named and linked), checked on the date shown. No affiliate links. If no live price was found, a $ band is shown as an editorial estimate. If no live price was seen for that exact model, the cell shows an editorial band, labelled “Editorial estimate”: $ under $250 · $$ $250–$600 · $$$ $600–$1,200 · $$$$ $1,200–$2,500 · $$$$$ over $2,500.

    Logos. Official brand marks are shown where the maker’s own site offers one (sources in icons/README.md); other brands appear as a plain text badge. Marks belong to their owners. Specs change, so always check the manufacturer’s current page.
